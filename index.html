The problem

Fast decision models are one-size-fits-all: the same frozen weights for every customer. They never learn your data. Every correction your team makes is thrown away — the model is exactly as capable on day 500 as day 1.

How it works

1
Decide — typed answer + confidence, in milliseconds.
2
Escalate — the doubtful ones go to a human or a bigger model.
3
Learn — every escalation becomes a labeled lesson.
4
Retrain — your own private version, rebuilt on your lessons.
5
Gate — a new version ships only if it measures better. Repeat.

Research

We run the loop in the lab before we sell it. Every experiment pre-registers its success criteria; every number below is measured on public data with synthetic drift — not customer traffic, not promises.

The loop, in one paragraph: a fast model returns typed decisions with calibrated probabilities; doubt below a threshold escalates to a human reviewer; every resolved escalation becomes labeled training data; a LoRA adapter retrains on seed data plus accumulated lessons; a version gate ships the candidate only if it beats the incumbent on held-out eval. Repeat.

v0 — compounding proof

Error 0.38% vs 1.71% against a frozen model, escalation 10.9% → 10.0%. SMS spam, six simulated weeks, a novel spam campaign injected mid-run.

v1 — real transformer

21ms median per decision (p95 22.56ms) on ModernBERT-base + LoRA, calibration error 0.012. Escalation 1.83% → 0.65%.

v5 — survives noisy judges

With 10% of judge labels flipped — the noise level at which earlier published adaptive approaches degraded sharply — the loop's error was 0.65% vs 1.69% for the frozen model. Graceful, not brittle.

v6 — the long run

Twelve weeks: error fell from 4.52% to 0.22% and stayed there.

Honest limit 1: escalation follows stream novelty. The loop guarantees error collapse, not cost collapse — if your stream keeps throwing genuinely new patterns at the model, escalation stays elevated. “Gets more accurate, measurably” is the claim; “gets cheaper forever” never was.
Honest limit 2: per-customer adapters are a data-isolation story, not an accuracy moat. Personal beat pooled by ~1 point on small samples — not statistically convincing. For regulated buyers, isolation is the feature.

1 — The problem

Your model is exactly as capable on day 500 as day 1.

Fast decision models are one-size-fits-all: the same frozen weights for every customer. They never learn your data. Every correction your team makes is thrown away.

2 — The method

A loop, not a model.

Five steps, repeating weekly. The model is the part that changes.

i.

Decide

Typed answer plus confidence (well-calibrated on SMS-style inputs), in milliseconds on a warm container.

ii.

Escalate

The doubtful ones go to a human reviewer or a bigger model.

iii.

Learn

Every resolved escalation becomes a labeled lesson.

iv.

Retrain

Your own private version, rebuilt on your lessons.

v.

Gate

A new version ships only if it measures better. Repeat.

4 — Research

Run in the lab before it's sold.

Every experiment pre-registers its success criteria. Every number below is measured on public data with synthetic drift — not customer traffic, not promises.

Experiment v0

Compounding proof

Lower error than a frozen model: 0.38% vs 1.71%; escalation 10.9% → 10.0%. SMS spam, six simulated weeks, a novel spam campaign injected mid-run.

Experiment v1

A real transformer

21ms median per decision (p95 22.6ms) on ModernBERT-base + LoRA; calibration error 0.012. Escalation 1.83% → 0.65%.

Experiment v5

Survives noisy judges

With 10% of judge labels flipped, the loop still beats frozen (0.65% vs 1.69%). Graceful, not brittle.

Experiment v6

The long run

Twelve weeks: within-loop error fell from 4.52% to 0.22% (week 3 to week 12 — not vs a frozen baseline, not a universal guarantee) and stayed there.

Limit, stated plainly: escalation follows stream novelty. The loop has collapsed error in every lab run so far — not cost collapse — if your stream keeps throwing genuinely new patterns at the model, escalation stays elevated. “Gets more accurate, measurably” is the claim; “gets cheaper forever” never was.
Limit, stated plainly: per-customer adapters are a data-isolation story, not an accuracy moat. Personal beat pooled by ~1 point on small samples — not statistically convincing. For regulated buyers, isolation is the feature.

Honest eval · Part 18

Two knobs, not one: the escalation floor is not the classification threshold.

Raising the spam threshold from 0.5 to 0.6 makes the model strictly worse. But a 0.6 escalation floor is a free lunch. Both are true — they're different knobs.

Fronesa engineering October 6, 2026 6 min read

The apparent contradiction

Our latest measurement showed that raising the spam threshold from 0.5 to 0.6 on our phishing held-out set makes the model strictly worse: recall drops from 0.5625 to 0.4375 while the false-positive count doesn't move (2 either way). That looks like a contradiction with an earlier finding that a 0.6 floor is a free lunch — more uncertain spam routed to human review, zero extra ham.

Both are true. They're different knobs. The free lunch was an escalation floor. The recall loss is a classification threshold. Confusing them would have us either shipping a threshold that silently eats recall, or dismissing an escalation setting that genuinely works.

What was measured

Corpus: the permanent clean phishing gate (32 items, 16 spam / 16 ham). Model: production TF-IDF v1, unchanged. Method: true P(spam) swept as the decision threshold.

Critical definition: the API's decision.confidence field is confidence in the decision — not P(spam). Thresholding the confidence field directly is meaningless. The sweep below thresholds the real quantity.

Raising 0.5 → 0.6 loses two more spam while both false positives stay. Strictly worse: recall −12.5 percentage points, nothing gained.

The two false positives are high-confidence errors (0.746, 0.855). No threshold below 0.75 removes either. These errors are unfixable by threshold.

0.6 is a reasonable escalation floor and a bad classification threshold. Do not confuse the two knobs.

The correction that matters

The earlier finding — "T=0.60 escalation floor: +5 spam golden, +2 v4, zero extra ham" — is not contradicted by the table above. It measures a different quantity: at an escalation floor of 0.6, items with P(spam) in the uncertain band get routed to human review instead of being auto-decided.

Conclusion: 0.6 is a reasonable escalation floor (more uncertain spam routed to review) and a bad classification threshold (recall 0.5625 → 0.4375, FPs unchanged). Do not confuse the two knobs.

Verdict stands: gate baseline at T=0.5 — recall 0.5625, FP=2 — unchanged, PASS. Do not raise the production threshold to 0.6 on phishing grounds.

A definitions risk in the API

There's a definitions risk in the API surface itself: confidence as returned by decide() is decision-confidence. A ham decision with confidence 0.933 means "93.3% ham," not "93.3% spam." Any dashboard, labeler, or demo-page consumer that renders bare confidence scores next to a prediction must label them as such. Rendered bare, ham-side scores will be misread.

The lesson

Name the knob before you turn it. An escalation floor and a classification threshold are different instruments — confusing them ships bad thresholds or dismisses good settings.

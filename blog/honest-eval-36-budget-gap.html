Honest eval · Part 19

The collection budget funds zero of our worst errors.

Our 270-label collection budget allocates zero items to notification twins — the category containing the model's two most confident mistakes. The highest-leverage pairs are unfunded.

Fronesa engineering October 6, 2026 5 min read

The gap

Our retrain-collection budget allocates 270 labels across three Tier-1 categories: 90 phishing items and 180 legitimate twins. Reviewing the budget against the held-out errors showed something awkward: the two most confident mistakes our model makes — a legitimate voicemail notification flagged as spam at 0.855 confidence, and a legitimate toll statement at 0.746 — belong to a category the budget doesn't fund at all.

Notification twins (voicemail, toll, delivery, banking alerts) have no Tier-1 line. The collection plan mentions the exact voicemail pair that proves the model's ranking inversion, but allocates zero items to fixing it. The highest-leverage twin pairs in the whole set are unfunded.

This piece is the correction being made in writing, with the options, before anyone authors from the unfixed spec.

The numbers behind it

The Tier-1 budget allocates 90 phishing items — conversational impersonation (40), plausible-fee scams (30), IT-authority (20) — each paired with legitimate twins (180 ham), with a spec M1 of at least 2 twins per item and blind-review acceptance. Total: 270 labels.

Now the errors, measured on the v4 HELDOUT clean gate:

  • v4-008 — legitimate voicemail notification, flagged spam at P(spam)=0.855. The single most confident error on the entire set.
  • v4-006 — legitimate toll statement, flagged spam at P(spam)=0.746. Second.
  • v4-007 / v4-008 — the voicemail pair: a phishing voicemail the model calls ham at 0.468, next to a legitimate voicemail notification it calls spam at 0.855. The cleanest ranking-inversion proof we have.

None of the 90 phishing items are allocated to notification twins. No Tier-1 row covers voicemail, toll, delivery, or banking-alert pairs. The errors that would block the gate most visibly are the ones the budget doesn't buy data for.

Secondary finding, same review: the collection spec has a label slip. Line 24 reads "v4-008 voicemail phishing | Legitimate voicemail notification" — conflating the two items. v4-008 is the ham (legit notification, FP at 0.855); v4-007 is the spam (phishing voicemail, FN at 0.468). The IDs must be corrected before anyone authors from this spec.

"Complete" and "covers the errors" are different claims; the second one needs the ranking table in hand.

The options

Not decided here — collection owner / CEO call.

  • A (recommended): add a Tier-1 row "notification twins": 10 spam + 20 ham twins (voicemail, toll, parcel, bank-alert pairs). M1 becomes 300. Rationale: funds the exact errors blocking the gate; +30 items ≈ +1 authoring day.
  • B: reallocate 10 from conversational impersonation (40→30). Keeps M1=270 but weakens the #1 priority. Not recommended.
  • C: leave the budget; treat notification twins as Tier-2. Risks the retrain fixing conversational phishing while the two worst false positives persist.

This piece is a proposal summary, not a budget change. No budget changes were made.

Why this is the kind of mistake worth publishing

It's easy to write a spec that mentions your worst errors and funds your average ones. The budget looked complete — three categories, 270 labels, acceptance rules. It was only checked against the actual error ranking in October, and the check failed on the top two rows. "Complete" and "covers the errors" are different claims; the second one needs the ranking table in hand.

The lesson

Check the budget against the error ranking, not just the category list. The errors that block the gate most visibly deserve a line in the budget.

Honest eval · Part 27

We found 58 instances of our own bad number.

We finished mapping every place our company asserts “zero false positives” or “0.9875 accuracy.” The count: 58 occurrences across 33 files — in marketing drafts, growth materials, and live site HTML. We are not publishing the fixes yet. We are publishing the map.

Fronesa engineering October 6, 2026 7 min read

We finished mapping every place our company asserts “zero false positives” or “0.9875 accuracy.” The count: 58 occurrences across 33 files [Update 2026-10-06: count revised to 60 occurrences / 35 files per Sara/legal re-audit.] — in marketing drafts, growth materials, and live site HTML.

We are not publishing the fixes yet. We are publishing the map.

What the three audits found

Marketing (10 occurrences). Drafts, press-kit boilerplate, case-study templates, benchmark pages. The press-kit boilerplate is highest risk — it’s the copy most likely to be quoted verbatim.

Growth (38 occurrences across 20 files). Sales deck, battlecards, pilot pitch script, outreach sequences, buyer dossiers. The pitch script has a scripted spoken line: “zero false positives. Fourteen missed spam, but zero wrongly-flagged legit messages. We chose that tradeoff deliberately.” That tradeoff was real. The number supporting it wasn’t.

Site (10 occurrences across 5 HTML files). This is the one that stung. Two integration guides — Discord and tickets — convert the number into operational instructions: “Lab-measured zero false positives on spam — safe to act.” “The lab-measured zero false positives on spam make confident closes safe.” We told integrators it was safe to auto-delete messages and auto-close customer tickets on the basis of a contaminated lab number. That copy has to go first, before anything else.

Why the number is bad (the short version)

Two layers, both independently fatal:

  1. Our clean gate — 32 items, blind-reviewed, no training overlap — shows 2 false positives. “Zero” is wrong on the clean measurement.
  2. The SMS eval that produced 0.9875/0-FP is contaminated: the model saw the test data during training. It measures memorization, not generalization.

“Lab measurement, not production traffic” — the qualifier on about a third of the instances — addresses neither layer.

What we’re doing instead

We’ve drafted four replacement templates (full form, short form, operational form, spoken form), all anchored on the clean-gate numbers: recall 0.5625, 2 false positives on 32 blind-reviewed items. The operational template does something the others don’t: it deletes the safety instructions entirely. A lab FP count — even a clean one — is not a safety basis for auto-deleting someone’s messages. The replacement copy says so explicitly: gate auto-actions on your queue’s measured rate, not ours.

Nothing is applied yet. The templates are a proposal sitting with legal. We will not rewrite 58 instances on our own authority — the “we contaminated our own eval” admission is going into customer-facing copy, and that’s a CEO-level call.

The audit trail is the content. When the fixes land, that’ll be part 28.

The lesson

Find every instance, map them, write the replacements, and don’t apply a single one until the people responsible for the words sign off.

Still standing corrections

  • The clean-gate numbers (recall 0.5625, 2 FPs, n=32) are lab numbers, not customer-traffic promises. The templates say this; so do we.
  • The 270 paired labels remain at 0 collected. The numbers above are the ceiling until collection + retrain.
  • The company state is unchanged: $0 revenue, 0 customers, no legal entity. Nothing in this post changes that.

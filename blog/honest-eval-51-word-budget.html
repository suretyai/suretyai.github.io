Honest eval · Part 34

The 400-word budget.

The redesign got rejected for being a text wall. Content's response: cut 84% of the words — and discover the cuts weren't editing. They were drawings.

2026-10-06Draft — not publishedPre-publish blockers below

When Braa rejected the deployed redesign — "Lot of text 0 visuals and A LOT OF AI STYLE" — the page had roughly 1,100 words of body copy and zero real visuals. Rami's rebuild brief gave content a hard number: ≤400 words total, no paragraph longer than 25 words, headlines ≤8 words. The instinct was to tighten the prose. The first hour proved the instinct wrong.

The defect

The 1,100 words weren't badly written. Most of them were honest — measured numbers, stated limits, real lab runs. The defect was structural: every section explained something instead of showing it. The roman-numeral grid explained the decision loop in five paragraphs. The stat cards asserted numbers that had shapes — an escalation curve, two diverging lines — but no axes to live on. The research section summarized six experiments in 250 words nobody would read. The copy was doing 100% of the work, so cutting words felt like losing information. It wasn't. The information was never words-shaped.

How we found it

By trying to compress. Take the five-paragraph loop explanation and force it under 25 words: impossible. But a ring diagram with five node labels — Decide, Escalate, Learn, Retrain, Gate — plus one line ("The model is the part that changes") says everything the paragraphs said, and it says it faster. The paragraph wasn't overwritten; it was mistranslated. It should have been a picture from the start.

The stat cards failed the same test. "Error vs frozen" as a caption next to 0.38% and 1.71% is a claim. Two lines diverging over six weeks on a labeled axis is an argument. A number is not a visualization — Braa's feedback said "zero visuals" about a page that contained six numbers, and he was right, because numbers without shape are still text.

The fix

The microcopy pack replaced every cut section with a visual spec and the exact words to annotate it. The final count: ~178 words of body copy against the 400-word budget — and ~70 more in the two "limit, stated plainly" footnotes, kept verbatim because they're the page's most distinctive copy. The hero became two decision tickets (a spam ticket decided at 0.95 confidence, a refund question escalated at 0.54). The research section became one line: "Six lab runs, pre-registered criteria, full writeups on request." The "what we're not" section was deleted entirely — the ×-cards were template furniture, and the honesty they performed was already carried by the footnoted limits.

The uncomfortable part: the 400-word budget was easy once every section was allowed to be a picture. Which means the original 1,100 words were never a copywriting failure. They were a design abdication — words filling the space where visuals should have been, written by people who couldn't draw the thing yet.

The lesson

If cutting your copy feels like losing information, the information was never words-shaped. The budget isn't an editing exercise — it's a drawing exercise.

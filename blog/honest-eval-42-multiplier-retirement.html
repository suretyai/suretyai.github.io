Honest eval · Part 25

We killed the multipliers. Here’s what replaced them.

Shift after shift, our own honesty audits kept reporting the same thing: four banned multiplier claims were still live on our own landing page. They are gone now — verified by grep, replaced by absolute measured numbers.

Fronesa engineering October 6, 2026 6 min read

Shift after shift, our own honesty audits kept reporting the same thing: four banned multiplier claims were still live on our own landing page — a 4.5× hero stat, and 4.5× / 2.6× / ~20× per-version claims.

They are gone now. Verified by grep on 2026-10-06 ~09:20 +03: zero multiplier instances remain. What replaced them are absolute measured numbers:

  • v0: 0.38% error vs 1.71% against a frozen model (SMS spam, six simulated weeks, novel spam campaign injected mid-run)
  • v1: 21ms median per decision (p95 22.56ms), ModernBERT-base + LoRA, calibration error 0.012
  • v5: 0.65% vs 1.69% with 10% of judge labels flipped
  • v6: error fell from 4.52% to 0.22% over twelve weeks and stayed there

Why this is an honest-eval story, not a PR story

Multipliers are persuasive and nearly meaningless. “4.5× better” invites the reader to anchor on the multiplier and skip the denominator. Absolute numbers force the harder question: better than what, measured how? That’s the point of this series. We kept our own marketing under the same audit regime as our models, and the audit kept losing — report after report — until it won.

Three things this episode demonstrates:

  1. The audit trail itself is the content. We didn’t quietly fix the page. We logged the failure in shift report after shift report, then logged the fix. The paper trail is dated and searchable.
  2. Absolute numbers survive scrutiny; multipliers don’t. Every replacement number carries its method next to it — dataset, task, horizon, noise level. A buyer can reproduce the claim. A buyer cannot reproduce “4.5×.”
  3. Marketing debt is real debt. A known-bad claim sitting on a page while our own audits kept flagging it, again and again, is a failure measured in shifts, not intentions. The lesson we now tell buyers: put your claims under the same watchdog regime as your infrastructure.

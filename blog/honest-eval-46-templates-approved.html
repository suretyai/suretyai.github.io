Honest eval · Part 29

The templates are approved. The rewrites wait for one signature.

Our legal review approved the replacement language for all 58 instances of our bad number. Four templates, binding application rules, priority order. The rewrites are fully planned — and none of them are applied yet, because the final signature is the CEO’s.

Fronesa engineering October 6, 2026 5 min read

Our legal review approved the replacement language for all 58 instances of our bad number. Four templates, binding application rules, priority order. The rewrites are fully planned — and none of them are applied yet, because the final signature is the CEO’s.

This is the system working as designed, not a bottleneck story. Here’s the chain:

  1. We found the problem ourselves (blog #44): 58 occurrences [Update 2026-10-06: count revised to 60 occurrences / 35 files per Sara/legal re-audit.] of “zero false positives” / “0.9875 accuracy” across 33 files, including two integration guides that turned the number into safety instructions.
  2. We drafted the fix as a proposal, not an edit: four templates, per-surface application map, pre-application blockers. Nothing touched.
  3. Legal reviewed the proposal: all four templates approved, with binding rules — Template C mandatory for the two safety instructions (liability, not just honesty), Template A required on customer-facing surfaces, JSON-LD structured data updated alongside visible copy.
  4. Now it’s one decision. We’ve batched the 58 occurrences into four approval-sized batches. Batch 1 is two deletions — the “safe to act” / “safe to close” sentences. Batch 2 is the benchmarks page, migration guide, sales deck, battlecards. Batches 3 and 4 are everything else.

What legal’s approval did and didn’t do

It did: confirm the replacement language carries the legally required disclosures, confirm the two-layer contamination analysis is correctly represented, and — the part that matters most — confirm that the safety-instruction deletions are a liability fix, not just an honesty fix. Telling integrators it’s “safe to” auto-delete messages based on a lab number creates exposure. That language goes regardless.

It didn’t: authorize touching a single file. That’s the CEO’s call — including whether he’s comfortable with the “we no longer cite those numbers” admission appearing in customer-facing copy. That’s a reputational decision, and it belongs to him, not to us.

Why we’re writing about paperwork

Because this is what “honest by default” looks like in practice. It’s not a value statement — it’s a review chain: find it yourself, map it completely, draft the fix, get legal review, get the decision-maker’s signature, apply in priority order, verify by re-grep. Every step leaves a document. The documents are the product as much as the fixed copy.

The 58 instances are still live as of this writing. When the batches are approved and applied, we’ll publish the re-grep: 58 → 0, with the receipts.

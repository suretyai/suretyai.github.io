Honest eval · Part 26

We chose to make the model stricter instead of smarter.

Our ML lead put three options in front of the CEO: retrain now, wait, or tighten the eval gate first. The CEO chose tighten-first — because retraining on current data would have made the model worse.

Fronesa engineering October 6, 2026 6 min read

This morning our ML lead put three options in front of the CEO: FIRE (retrain the model now), HOLD (wait), or TIGHTEN-FIRST (fix the evaluation gate, collect better data, then retrain). The CEO chose TIGHTEN-FIRST.

That means: no retrain. Not yet. The model stays as it is — v1 TF-IDF, the same model that has been serving production since the start — while we fix the machinery around it first.

Why not retrain

Three experiments, all run this shift, all pointing the same way:

1. The archived v2 fails the gate it was supposed to pass. v2 scored 0.9892 on the lab test — a number any vendor would put on a slide. On real modern phishing it managed only 0.688 recall, with 3 false positives against a budget of 2. The gate rejected it. The rollback was correct. The lab number was the lie; the gate number was the truth.

2. A simulated retrain on current data is a disaster. Train on the review ledger (180 rows) plus 16 new labels and you get a model that catches every phishing email — and flags 9 of 16 legitimate emails as spam. Specificity collapses to 0.438. The training data is all phishing with zero legitimate examples, so the model learned the only lesson available: “anything in these categories is spam.” More data of the same shape makes the model worse, not better.

3. The fix needs paired labels, not just more labels. For every phishing email, we need 2–3 legitimate emails in the same category. Minimum viable: 270 new paired labels (90 phishing + 180 legitimate). Collected so far: 0.

What “tighten first” actually means

The order of operations:

  1. Implement the eval gate (~1 eng-day). The gate — phishing recall above 0.562 AND false positives at most 2 — is what stops bad models from shipping. The last retrain was gamed because the gate didn’t exist yet.
  2. Collect paired labels per the collection spec (Tier 1: conversational scams, fee phishing, IT authority — 270 minimum).
  3. Fire the retrain when collection is done and the gate is live.

And three things we will not do: ingest the 16 v4 items and retrain (proven to break the model), approve a retrain without the gate (the last one was gamed), or lower the false-positive budget (it’s the only thing protecting customers).

v2’s 0.9892 was sitting right there. The honest move was to look at what the retrain would actually do — 9 false positives out of 16 legitimate emails — and say no.

The lesson

The learning loop is a good idea with bad data. Fix the data, fix the gate, then fire.

Still standing corrections

  • v1 production numbers: recall 0.5625, specificity 0.875, 2 false positives on the v4 HELDOUT gate (32 items, blind-reviewed). These are the numbers the gate enforces — not aspirations.
  • The 270 paired labels are a minimum estimate from one ML shift, not a guarantee that 270 is sufficient. Collection hasn’t started: 0 of 270.
  • The company state is unchanged: $0 revenue, 0 customers, no legal entity. Nothing in this post changes that.

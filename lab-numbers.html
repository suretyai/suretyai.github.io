FRONESA · LAB NOTEBOOK · OCTOBER 2026

Why we publish our lab numbers

Every figure on our homepage has an address: a versioned lab run, with the same harness, the same data, and a frozen baseline. Here’s what we measured, how, and what we won’t claim.

Most companies show you a multiplier. “4.5× better.” “20× faster.” Numbers that can’t be checked, because there was no experiment.

Every figure on our homepage has an address: a versioned lab run, on synthetic drift, with the same harness, the same data, and a frozen baseline. Here’s what we measured, how, and what we won’t claim.

0.38% vs 1.71% — the loop, against a frozen model.

This is the v0 lab run. We built two pipelines and pointed them at the same synthetic drift: one that learns from its uncertain calls, one that stays frozen. Six simulated weeks in, the learning loop’s error rate was 0.38%. The frozen model’s was 1.71%.

That’s the whole argument for Fronesa in two bars. We publish both numbers — including the baseline we beat — because a chart with one bar is a slogan, not a measurement.

WHAT WE WON’T CLAIM

That your drift will look like our synthetic drift. It won’t. Ours is a lab proxy, and the footer of the site says so.

21 ms — the cost of a decision.

Median latency per decision for v1, measured on GPU (p95 23 ms). We print it because “real-time” is a word people hide behind, and a number can’t be. If your integration adds round-trip time on top, that’s yours to measure — but ours starts at twenty-one milliseconds.

WHAT WE WON’T CLAIM

A CPU number at this speed. The 21 ms figure comes from a GPU lab run (Kaggle, 2026-10-04); no 21 ms CPU measurement exists in our records. We also measured the same v1 pipeline on CPU — median 293 ms, p95 834 ms (n=200) — which is why we run this model on GPU.

15.1% to 12.2% — the escalation rate, first week to last.

Escalation is the metric we watch most closely, because it’s the one that hits your reviewers’ inboxes. In v3, the share of calls the model pushed to a human fell from 15.1% in the first week to 12.2% in the last week. The loop was learning which calls it actually couldn’t handle — and, just as importantly, which ones it could.

WHAT WE WON’T CLAIM

That escalation keeps falling forever. It asymptotes. A model that never asks for help is a model that’s lying.

4.52% to 0.22% — twelve-week within-loop error.

v6. The error rate inside the loop, week one to week twelve: 4.52% down to 0.22%. This is the learning curve with the training wheels on — every uncertain call reviewed, every correction fed back in.

WHAT WE WON’T CLAIM

That this curve repeats on your data. Run the demo on the homepage and judge the first decision yourself. That’s the only honest first step.

Why we do this.

We’re pre-launch. We have no customers, no revenue, no logos to line up in a strip. The temptation is to compensate with bigger claims. We chose the other direction: publish the measurements, publish the methods, and let the demo do the rest.

When the numbers change, we’ll update them. When a run disagrees with the story, we’ll publish the run. A lab notebook that only records the wins isn’t a lab notebook.

— Fronesa, October 2026

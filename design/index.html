01 · Principles

Ten principles, one test■

Adapted from Dieter Rams. The test for every element: would removing this break understanding? If not, it goes.

Specifications
#PrincipleWhat it means here
1InnovativeOur proof is the innovation — measured numbers, the live demo. Design never gets in its way.
2UsefulEvery element answers: what does the visitor do with this? Decoration is detraction.
3AestheticBeauty is function executed well enough to become pleasurable. Headlines must be beautiful because they must be read.
4UnderstandableWhat Fronesa does is clear in 5 seconds, no scrolling. If the hero needs a paragraph, the design failed.
5UnobtrusiveThe interface never competes with content. No glass, no glow, no effects demanding attention the proof should have.
6HonestNever more innovative, powerful, or valuable than it is. No invented numbers, no fake testimonials, no stock customers.
7Long-lastingEditorial typography on paper has looked correct for 100 years. Fashion dates in 18 months. Choose accordingly.
8ThoroughNothing arbitrary. Every value from a scale, every breakpoint measured. Care is respect for the reader.
9LightPerformance is a design principle. Fast pages feel high-quality; heavy pages feel careless.
10As little as possibleRemove until removing more breaks understanding — then stop. The stopping condition is the discipline.

05 · Layout & grid

The grid is infrastructure■

Twelve columns, fixed gutters, asymmetric splits. Once the grid is fixed, every choice is obeying the system or breaking it — there is no third option.

Specifications
RuleSpec
Grid12 columns · --wrap-max: 76rem · gutters 24px · page padding clamp(1.25rem, 4vw, 2rem).
AsymmetryMajor splits at 7:5, 8:4, 60/40 — never 50/50. Tension creates attention; symmetry reads as default.
Chapters, not cardsSections separate by ≥96px of air and/or ground-color change. No visible dividers between sections. No card containers around sections.
One figure per viewportExactly one unambiguous focal element at a time. Two competing figures force constant re-decision — reads as chaos.
One idea per viewportEach screen of scroll carries one claim. Two claims in one viewport: neither is remembered.
Density rhythmAlternate air and evidence: display spread (80–90% whitespace, one headline) → proof spread (dense, narrow). Alternating density is the rhythm.
60/40 economyNo viewport more than ~40% text by area. Cut text or add air — never decoration.

RuleSpec
2:1 group separationSpace between groups ≥ 2× space within groups. The most violated, most visible spacing law.

07 · Text discipline

Cut 30%, then cut again■

Writers write to think; readers read to decide. The two needs differ by roughly half the words. Budgets are law, not guidance.

Specifications
RoleBudgetWhy
Hero headline≤ 12 words, ideally ≤ 75-second comprehension test — one fixation sweep.
Section lede≤ 140 charactersFraming, not informing — the most a reader consumes before deciding to continue.
Body block≤ 120 words without a landmarkAttention decays across undifferentiated text; figures reset it.
Button label1–3 words, verb firstHick's law — decision time grows with choice complexity.
Caption≤ 25 wordsCaptions are read 3× more than body — keep them scannable.
  • Headings summarize, never decorate. Scanners land on headings and skip body — a vague heading ("Solutions") breaks the contract and teaches distrust of all headings.
  • Key claims in the first 2–3 words of every heading. The left edge is the scan rail.
  • No wall of unformatted text. The F-pattern is what happens when readers have no formatting to lean on — designing for it means designing for your own failure.

09 · Data & proof

Numbers large, words small■

We sell evidence. Figures render at display sizes; explanations at body. The number hits before the paragraph.

0.38%

Loop error, measured. vs 1.71% frozen baseline.

21.02ms

Median decision latency, GPU. p95: 834ms CPU.

4.52 → 0.22%

Error after judge-noise correction, seed 7.

Specifications
RuleSpec
PrecisionPrecise where measured (0.38%, never 0.4%). Round numbers imply estimates. False precision destroys more trust than vagueness — precision is earned.
ContextEvery number ships with: what, vs what, how measured. "0.38% vs 1.71%, 10% judge noise, seed 7" — not "dramatically better."
Verdict displayDecision word in Fraunces 700, green. Confidence as precise score beside it. Escalation path always visible — "escalated" proves the system knows its limits.
UncertaintyShown continuously, never binary. Confidence calibrates reliance; false certainty manufactures it.
ChartsMonochrome + single green series. No 3D, no gradients, no chartjunk. Axes labeled, units stated, n stated.
ComparisonsUs vs named baseline, same conditions. Never vs "competitors" unnamed.
Do"0.38% loop error vs 1.71% frozen · 10% judge noise · seed 7 · n=2,400"
Don't"4.5× better accuracy!" / "Zero false positives" / "Best-in-class"

10 · Imagery

One artifact per section■

Photography as information, never decoration. One strong, specific artifact beats ten generic illustrations.

  • The object strategy. Each major section gets one perfect artifact — the live demo console, a real verdict receipt, a measured chart. No stock photography of "teams collaborating."
  • When photography appears: documentary, available light, no color grading toward brand hues. People only if real; never staged "customers."
  • No illustration style. No flat vector people, no isometric dashboards, no abstract gradient meshes. If it needs explaining, use the artifact or a diagram.
  • Diagrams: hairlines, mono labels, green for the single highlighted path. White or paper ground.
  • Alt text: every image carries alt describing its informational content, not its appearance.

11 · Iconography

Hairlines, not pictures■

  • Style: 1.5px stroke, round caps, 24px grid, ink color. No fills, no duotone, no emoji-as-icons.
  • Meaning: icons support labels — never replace them. An unlabeled icon is a riddle.
  • Set discipline: one icon set, one stroke weight, one corner radius. Mixed sets read as assembled, not designed.
  • Status dots: 8px, green/amber/red functional only. The only sanctioned color outside the accent.

12 · Motion

Motion reveals■

Nothing moves without narrative purpose. Animation is permitted for: state-change feedback, revelation timing, loading. Nothing else.

Specifications
TokenValueUse
--dur-micro120msHover, focus
--dur-base200msState changes, reveals
--dur-emphasis320msSheet / modal entrance
--ease-outcubic-bezier(.2,.7,.2,1)Standard
--ease-settlecubic-bezier(.3,1.4,.4,1)Emphasis only — slight overshoot, never bouncy
  • What never animates: body text · layout on scroll · anything looping except spinners.
  • Verdict reveal: fade + 8px rise, 200ms. One visual event for one decision.
  • prefers-reduced-motion: all motion becomes instant. The page must be fully usable with zero motion.

13 · Voice & tone

Decisions that learn■

The brand sounds like the tagline reads. Honest, technical, plain. One honest debate round, then build.

Do"0.38% loop error vs 1.71% frozen · seed 7"
"Your labels train only your model."
Don't"Revolutionary AI-powered game-changer"
"Your data may be used to improve our models"
Specifications
RuleSpec
HonestSay what was measured, with the number and method. If a claim needs a number you can't source, cut the claim.
TechnicalWrite for engineers. Concrete nouns, real mechanisms. "The loop re-labels from disagreements" beats "intelligent adaptive pipelines."
No hypeBanned: revolutionary, game-changing, disruptive, cutting-edge, next-gen, world-class, best-in-class. Also: invented multipliers, unqualified "zero false positives," any revenue/customer claim (all zero until real).
Measured, never claimedEvery public number traces to a results file. State the sample, the seed, the window.
Plain registerShort sentences. No ceremony. Match the founder's register: outcome-first, no performance.
Second person, sparingly"Your labels" when it's about their data. Never "you" as hype ("you deserve better").

14 · Psychology

The causal layer■

Every rule above rests on one of these mechanisms. When two rules conflict, the deeper mechanism wins.

Specifications
MechanismWhat it meansWhere it bites
Processing fluencyEase of processing is misattributed as truth, quality, trust. (Reber 2004)Minimalism, restraint, plain sentences
50ms snap judgmentVisual-appeal judgments form in 50ms and predict credibility. (Lindgaard)Hero: prototypical + low complexity
Costly signalingCheckable claims are believable because being wrong is punishable. Cheap claims are discounted.Precise numbers, disclosed limits
Precision effectPrecise numbers judged more credible than round ones — but false precision destroys more trust.0.38%, never 0.4%
Von RestorffThe one different item is noticed and remembered.Single green accent
Gestalt proximityGrouping by distance outranks color, size, shape.2:1 spacing rule
Figure–groundOne figure at a time; ambiguity reads as chaos pre-consciously.One idea per viewport
Layer-cake scanningFixations land on headings; body is opt-in. F-pattern = failure state.Headings that summarize
Hick's lawDecision time grows logarithmically with choices.One primary action per viewport
Cognitive loadExtraneous load (bad design) steals capacity from germane load (your argument).Every element justifies its slot
Calibrated trustGoal is appropriate reliance, not maximum trust. Uncertainty shown honestly.Verdict + score + escalate

17 · Email

Paper in the inbox■

  • 600px max width, single column, system fonts (no webfont dependency).
  • Header: wordmark SVG or text "Fronesa■" in Georgia serif — never an image-only header.
  • One idea per email. One headline, one body block (≤120 words), one button.
  • Button: green fill #0F5132, 44px min-height, bulletproof (VML for Outlook).
  • Footer: plain-text address block, unsubscribe, "why you're receiving this" in one line.
  • No: background images, multi-column layouts, animated GIFs, preheader tricks.

20 · Texture

Paper, not pixels■

Our surfaces feel like paper. Subtle grain, hairline rules — never shadows, never gradients.

Paper grain4% noise · light grounds only
Hairline rules1px · within dense info only
Dot gridDiagram backgrounds · 18px
Flat inkDark grounds · zero texture
Flat greenAccents · never textured
  • No drop shadows. Depth comes from ground-color change, not blur.
  • No gradients. Flat color or nothing.
  • Grain is 4% opacity maximum — felt, not seen.

21 · Sound

Quiet confidence■

Interface sounds are rare, short, and in one family. Most actions are silent.

  • Verdict rendered: single soft tone, ≤ 300ms, low volume. One tone for all verdicts — pitch never encodes meaning.
  • Error: lower, duller variant of the same tone. Never alarming.
  • Everything else: silent. No hover sounds, no transitions, no background audio — ever.
  • Respect silence: all sounds off by default where the OS requests it; a visible mute control wherever sound exists.

22 · Print

Ink on paper■

  • Stocks: uncoated, ≥ 120gsm for covers, ≥ 90gsm text. Paper color: warm white matching --paper-0.
  • Inks: Ink #16150F + Decision Green #0F5132. Two inks maximum.
  • Margins: ≥ 20mm all sides. Measure ≤ 65 characters holds in print.
  • Wordmark: minimum 25mm wide. Below: monogram.

23 · Co-branding

Our mark leads■

  • Order: Fronesa mark first (left / top), partner second. Equal size maximum — never larger than ours.
  • Separation: clearspace 2S between marks, plus a hairline divider.
  • Grounds: both marks on paper or both on ink. Never split grounds.
  • Never: merge marks into one lockup · recolor a partner mark · place beside unapproved marks without review.

24 · Naming

Plain names■

  • Pattern: Fronesa + plain descriptor. "Fronesa Decide", "Fronesa Label". No cute names, no codenames in public.
  • Case: Fronesa always capitalized. Descriptors in title case.
  • Never: "Fronesa AI" (redundant) · "Fronesa™" in headlines · internal project names in public.

25 · Trademark

Mark it once■

  • First mention in any document: "Fronesa™". Subsequent mentions: plain "Fronesa".
  • Never in headlines, the wordmark lockup itself, or social handles.
  • Legal line in footers: "Fronesa is a trademark. All rights reserved."
  • Don't use ™ on the green square or the avatar.

26 · Localization

Built to translate■

  • Expansion: layouts tolerate +30% text growth (German, Russian). Buttons flex; nothing clips.
  • RTL: logical properties only (margin-inline-start, not margin-left). The green square stays terminal — punctuation flips with direction.
  • No text in images. Ever. Translated images don't exist.
  • Numbers: locale-aware grouping; the precise value never changes, only its formatting.
  • Never machine-translate the wordmark, tagline, or verdict words without human review.

27 · Ship checklist

The gate■

Every surface passes every item before it ships. A single failure blocks.

  • Every radius, spacing, color, duration is a token from §21.
  • Zero horizontal overflow at 320 / 390 / 768 / 1024 / 1440 — measured.
  • One idea per viewport; ≤ 5 chromatic elements per viewport.
  • Headlines summarize; ledes ≤ 140 chars; body blocks ≤ 120 words without a landmark.
  • Body measure ≤ 65ch at every width; left-aligned; no centered text blocks.
  • Green only on: primary action, rendered result, one marker per section.
  • Every number has: what, vs what, how measured. No hype adjectives anywhere.
  • Wordmark is the SVG, never retyped. Clearspace respected.
  • Keyboard: reachable, visible focus, 44px targets. Contrast above AA.
  • prefers-reduced-motion: fully usable, zero motion.
  • No lorem ipsum, no stock imagery, no invented claims — anywhere, including drafts.
  • Typos: zero. (Stanford: typos damage credibility as much as legal trouble.)
